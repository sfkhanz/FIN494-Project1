Executive Summary & Research Question Author: Saif Khan Course: FIN 494 Execution Route: Synthetic Teaching & Development Fixture (synthetic) Snapshot Observation Date: December 31, 2025 Core Research Question: How does adding a liquidity requirement to a size-based common-stock universe change its composition, market capitalization profile, and execution risk? This study audits the systematic transition from an unconstrained equity extract to an institutional-grade investment universe. Using a standardized snapshot from December 31, 2025, we evaluate the impact of imposing a secondary 20-session liquidity hurdle (\(\text{ADV}_{20} \ge \$1.0\text{M/day}\)) onto a baseline universe filtered solely by primary security classification and a size threshold (\(\text{Market Cap} \ge \$1,000\text{M}\)). The secondary liquidity gate eliminates \(50.0\%\) of eligible baseline securities (dropping from 8 securities to 4 securities) and reduces issuer representation from 6 firms to 3 firms. While the median market capitalization of qualifying firms remains stable at \(\$1,550\text{M}\), the median 20-session average daily dollar volume (\(\text{ADV}_{20}\)) increases by \(50.0\%\), from \(\$1.50\text{M}\) to \(\$2.25\text{M}\). Imposing liquidity constraints effectively eliminates illiquid micro-cap “size outliers,” preventing high market-impact costs in execution while concentrating capital into seasoned issues with robust trading frequency.

Screening Methodology & Logic Hierarchy The screening architecture evaluates securities sequentially to ensure single-attribution exclusion accounting. Mathematical Formulations Equity Market Capitalization: \[\text{MarketCap}_i = \frac{|\text{Price}_{i, T}| \times \text{SharesOutstanding}_{i, T}}{10^6} \quad (\text{USD Millions})\] Threshold: \(\text{MarketCap}_i \ge \$1,000\text{M}\). Daily Dollar Volume Proxy: \[\text{DailyDollarVol}_{i, t} = \frac{\text{Price}_{i, t} \times \text{Volume}_{i, t}}{10^6} \quad (\text{USD Millions})\] Evaluated on shared market sessions where \(\text{Price}_{i, t} > 0\) and \(\text{Volume}_{i, t} \ge 0\). 20-Session Average Daily Dollar Volume (\(\text{ADV}_{20}\)): \[\text{ADV}_{20, i} = \frac{1}{N_i} \sum_{t=1}^{N_i} \text{DailyDollarVol}_{i, t}\] Threshold: \(\text{ADV}_{20, i} \ge \$1.00\text{M/day}\), conditioned on Ni15 valid trading sessions. Sequential Exclusion Hierarchy Input: Broad Security Extract (N = 16) │ ├── Step 1: Check Classification & Exchange Legitimacy │ └── Exclude if Share Code != 10/11 or Exchange != Major US (NYSE/NASDAQ/AMEX) │ ├── Step 2: Evaluate Primary Size Hurdle │ └── Exclude if Market Cap is Missing, Negative, or < $1,000M │ └── Output: Baseline Size Universe (N = 8) │ ├── Step 3: Audit Session Window Data Quality │ └── Exclude if Valid Trading Sessions N_valid < 15 │ └── Step 4: Evaluate Secondary Liquidity Hurdle └── Exclude if ADV_20 is Missing or < $1.00M / day └── Output: Final Investable Universe (N = 4)

Methodological Limitations & Historical Extension Plan When expanding this snapshot screening methodology to a dynamic, long-horizon historical panel (e.g., 2000–2025 CRSP monthly files), three structural biases must be addressed: Permanent Identifier Integrity: Using ticker symbols or CUSIP identifiers causes look-ahead and survivorship distortion due to corporate renamings, reorganizations, and identifier reassignment. The production pipeline must standardize on CRSP permanent security keys (PERMNO) and permanent company keys (PERMCO). Point-in-Time Conditioning & Delisting Returns: Historical cross-sections must be constructed strictly with information available at market close on date t. For securities that exit the sample mid-window due to mergers, bankruptcy, or liquidations, delisting returns (DLRET) and trailing volume adjustments must be integrated to prevent positive survivorship bias. Dynamic Rebalancing Cadence & Turnover Drag: While this cross-section reflects a single snapshot, production models require regular rebalancing (e.g., monthly or quarterly). Implementing buffer bands around the \(\$1,000\text{M}\) cap and \(\$1.00\text{M}\) ADV cutoffs is required to prevent portfolio whipsaw, where securities fluctuating around the threshold trigger excessive trading turnover and transaction fee erosion.
